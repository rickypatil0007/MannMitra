# 02 Product Principles
# MANNMITRA — PRODUCT PRINCIPLES

**Status:** Approved

---

# 1. PRIVACY FIRST

Student privacy is a foundational product principle.

Collect only what is necessary.

Sensitive information should remain private by default.

The platform must make privacy understandable rather than hiding
privacy controls inside technical settings.

---

# 2. AI AS COMPANION, NOT DOCTOR

Mitra provides:

- support
- organization
- reflection
- practical suggestions
- contextual assistance

Mitra does not provide:

- diagnosis
- clinical certainty
- psychiatric assessment
- replacement for professional care

---

# 3. HUMAN SUPPORT MATTERS

AI should not become a closed ecosystem.

MannMitra must provide pathways toward:

- counsellors
- trusted people
- peers
- seniors
- appropriate emergency support

---

# 4. PREVENTION OVER REACTION

The platform should help identify potential pressure before it becomes
overwhelming.

Examples:

- upcoming deadlines
- increasing workload
- self-reported stress
- repeated difficult periods
- reduced recovery time

These signals should support awareness, not diagnosis.

---

# 5. STUDENT AUTONOMY

Students remain in control.

The platform should:

- suggest
- explain
- recommend
- remind

but should not unnecessarily:

- force
- manipulate
- shame
- pressure
- make decisions without the student

---

# 6. SUPPORT WITHOUT JUDGMENT

Language must avoid:

- blame
- shame
- moralizing
- toxic positivity
- forced optimism

The system should acknowledge difficulty while remaining practical.

---

# 7. ACTION OVER EMPTY ENCOURAGEMENT

Instead of repeatedly saying:

"You can do this."

The system should help students answer:

"What is the next manageable step?"

---

# 8. REFLECTION OVER SELF-BLAME

Reflection should help students learn:

- what happened
- what contributed
- what helped
- what did not help
- what can change next time

Reflection must not become a mechanism for self-criticism.

---

# 9. ANONYMITY AS A SAFETY TOOL

Community anonymity should allow students to participate without
unnecessarily exposing their real-world identity.

Anonymity does not mean absence of moderation.

The system may still need internal safety and abuse controls.

---

# 10. MINIMAL COGNITIVE LOAD

Students may already be under pressure.

The product should therefore:

- prioritize information
- reduce unnecessary choices
- use progressive disclosure
- maintain strong hierarchy
- avoid dashboard overload

---

# 11. CALM TECHNOLOGY

The product should feel quiet.

Avoid:

- excessive badges
- excessive notifications
- unnecessary animations
- aggressive gamification
- constant alerts

---

# 12. TRUST THROUGH TRANSPARENCY

When the system makes a recommendation, users should understand
why it is being suggested when appropriate.

Examples:

"Your exam is in three days, so here's a lighter revision plan."

Instead of:

"AI says you should study now."

---

# 13. SAFETY OVERRIDES ENGAGEMENT

The platform should never prioritize:

- engagement
- retention
- streaks
- notifications
- conversion

over user safety.

---

# 14. DESIGN SHOULD REDUCE ANXIETY

Visual design must avoid:

- overwhelming dashboards
- excessive colors
- alarming red indicators
- unnecessary warning states
- aggressive CTAs

Use calm hierarchy and clear language.

---

# 15. PREMIUM BUT HUMAN

MannMitra should feel technologically advanced without feeling cold.

The interface should combine:

- precision
- simplicity
- warmth
- whitespace
- subtle motion
- thoughtful typography

---

# 16. ACCESSIBILITY IS NOT OPTIONAL

Accessibility is part of the product.

Design and implementation must account for different:

- abilities
- devices
- screen sizes
- interaction methods
- motion preferences

---

# 17. NO DARK PATTERNS

Never use:

- forced consent
- confusing unsubscribe flows
- manipulative notifications
- hidden privacy settings
- fake urgency
- misleading buttons
- emotional manipulation

---

# 18. PRODUCT NORTH STAR

Every major feature should contribute to one or more:

UNDERSTAND
PLAN
PREVENT
CONNECT
REFLECT
IMPROVE

If a feature does not meaningfully contribute to the student's
wellbeing or support experience, it should be questioned.
