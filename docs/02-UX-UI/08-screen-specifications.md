# 08 Screen Specifications
# MANNMITRA — SCREEN SPECIFICATIONS

Status: Core UX Specification

This document defines the visual and interaction requirements for
major MannMitra screens.

---

# 1. LANDING PAGE

## Goal

Explain MannMitra immediately and establish trust.

## Visual Direction

Extremely clean.

White background.

Large typography.

Subtle green accent.

Minimal imagery.

Generous whitespace.

---

## Hero

Headline should communicate:

AI-powered support for student wellbeing.

Supporting text should explain:

MannMitra helps students understand stress, organize responsibilities,
reflect, connect, and access support.

Primary CTA:

Get Started

Secondary CTA:

Explore MannMitra

---

## Visual

Use a premium product visualization.

Do not use generic stock mental-health imagery.

Avoid:

- smiling therapy stock photos
- brain illustrations
- excessive gradients
- cartoon illustrations

---

# 2. REGISTRATION

## Goal

Make account creation feel extremely simple.

Layout:

Centered content.

Large whitespace.

Minimal form.

Fields should be introduced progressively.

Primary CTA:

Create account

Secondary:

Sign in

---

# 3. ONBOARDING

Onboarding should feel calm and personal.

Do not create a long questionnaire.

Use progressive steps.

Each screen should answer:

Why are we asking this?

---

# 4. STUDENT DASHBOARD

## Goal

Provide immediate clarity.

---

## Top

Greeting:

"Good morning, [Name]."

Secondary contextual statement.

Example:

"Let's make today manageable."

---

## Primary Card

Today's focus.

One primary recommendation.

Example:

"You have a busy week ahead. Let's start with your exam on Friday."

Primary CTA:

Plan my week

---

## Wellness

Small wellness check-in.

Example:

"How are you feeling today?"

Use simple interaction.

Do not make it clinical.

---

## Tasks

Show:

- highest priority
- upcoming deadline
- completion state

Do not show every task.

---

## Mitra

Compact assistant card.

Example:

"Want help planning today?"

CTA:

Talk to Mitra

---

# 5. MITRA CHAT

## Visual

Large clean conversational canvas.

White background.

Subtle message separation.

Minimal chat bubbles.

---

## Composer

Rounded but restrained input.

Placeholder:

"What's on your mind?"

Optional quick actions:

Plan something
Talk it through
Reflect
Help me focus

---

## AI RESPONSE

Responses should have:

- readable width
- comfortable line height
- optional contextual actions

Example:

"That sounds like a lot to hold at once."

Then:

"Would it help if we broke the week into smaller steps?"

Actions:

Plan it
Talk about it

---

# 6. PLANNER

Planner should feel closer to a premium productivity tool
than a traditional academic portal.

Sections:

Today
Upcoming
Tasks
Calendar

---

## Task

Each task should show:

- title
- deadline
- priority
- completion state

Avoid visual clutter.

---

# 7. CALENDAR

Use a clean calendar.

Highlight:

- today
- important deadlines
- selected date

Use green selectively.

Do not color every event differently.

---

# 8. WELLNESS

Wellness should feel calm.

Top:

"How have you been feeling?"

Then:

Check-in
Stress
Mood
Trends

---

## STRESS

Show simple history.

Do not create medical-looking dashboards.

Use clear language:

"Your stress has been higher this week."

Not:

"Stress severity: 78%."

---

# 9. STRESS FORECAST

Forecast should communicate possibility.

Example:

"Friday may feel busier because you have two deadlines."

CTA:

Plan around it

---

# 10. REFLECTION

Use an uncluttered writing environment.

Prompt:

"What felt difficult today?"

Secondary:

"What helped?"

Then:

"What would you try differently next time?"

---

# 11. COMMUNITY

Community should feel safe and calm.

Feed:

- post
- anonymous identity
- timestamp
- reactions
- comments

Avoid engagement-heavy social media design.

Do not prioritize virality.

---

# 12. ANONYMOUS POST

Clearly explain:

"Your real name won't be shown to other community members."

Then:

What's on your mind?

CTA:

Post anonymously

---

# 13. SENIOR STORIES

Use editorial-style cards.

Each story should feel personal.

Display:

- topic
- author type
- year / context if appropriate
- short excerpt

Avoid gamified content cards.

---

# 14. COUNSELLOR

The counsellor experience should feel trustworthy.

Show:

- counsellor name
- qualification where appropriate
- availability
- support areas
- booking/request action

Avoid ranking counsellors like social media profiles.

---

# 15. TRUSTED CONTACTS

Explain the feature before asking users to configure it.

Example:

"Choose someone you trust who can be contacted through supported
safety workflows."

CTA:

Add trusted contact

---

# 16. SOS

SOS must be visually distinct from ordinary wellness features.

The interface should immediately communicate:

You are in an urgent support flow.

Provide clear support choices.

Avoid decorative UI.

Avoid confusing navigation.

Exact emergency behavior is defined in:

06-HUMAN-SUPPORT/05-sos-system.md

and

06-HUMAN-SUPPORT/07-emergency-safety.md

---

# 17. PROFILE

Keep profile simple.

Sections:

Personal
Preferences
Privacy
Notifications
Data
Account

---

# 18. PRIVACY CENTER

Students should clearly see:

What data exists?

Who can access it?

What have I consented to?

What can I delete?

---

# 19. NOTIFICATIONS

Notifications should be grouped by purpose.

Avoid endless lists.

Allow:

- notification preferences
- quiet periods
- category controls

---

# 20. EMPTY STATES

Every major screen must have a designed empty state.

Never leave blank white space with no explanation.

---

# 21. ERROR STATES

Every major workflow must have an error state.

Errors should tell the user:

What happened?

What can I do?

---

# 22. LOADING STATES

Every asynchronous screen must have a loading experience.

Avoid layout jumps.

---

# 23. OVERALL SCREEN RULE

Every screen must maintain:

ONE CLEAR PURPOSE
ONE PRIMARY ACTION
STRONG HIERARCHY
GENERous WHITESPACE
CALM COLOR
MINIMAL DECORATION

---

# FINAL DESIGN TEST

Before approving a screen, ask:

Does this look premium?

Does it feel calm?

Can a stressed student understand it quickly?

Is the primary action obvious?

Is the page too crowded?

Is green being overused?

Are the icons consistent?

Does it work on mobile?

Can it be used with a keyboard?

Does it feel like MannMitra rather than a generic AI dashboard?

If the answer is no, redesign the screen.
